Her story

Off the conveyor belt.

Abigail Tracey did the sensible thing. She trained as a lawyer in the UK, and the path ahead was all laid out.

It’s a very particular kind of path. You work hard, you pass the next thing, and the next thing arrives. Nobody asks you whether you want it, because it’s so obviously good. It carries you along like a conveyor belt, and for a long time that feels a lot like getting somewhere.

Then life stopped being sensible. Her father had a brain aneurysm. Her mother had breast cancer.

Nothing on a career plan prepares you for that. When illness comes into a family, it rearranges the questions. The ones that seemed urgent go quiet, and the ones you’d been putting off get very loud.

What is all this actually for?

Abigail finished her training. And then she turned her back on it all, booked a flight to India, and has never looked back.

Stepping off is the easy part to tell. The harder part is what comes afterwards, when nobody is telling you what the next thing is, and you have to work out for yourself what a good life might look like. It turns out that’s a question worth a lifetime, and one you can’t answer alone.

So she started asking. Not gurus, and not people selling five easy steps, but the brightest women she can find: scholars, artists, musicians and makers, women who have spent their lives thinking hard about love, work, the body and the mind.

That’s what A Brighter Humanity is. One question, asked across academics, music, art, fashion, love and life:

What makes a human life actually flourish?

She doesn’t claim to have the answer. She just jumped off the belt to go and look for it, and you’re welcome to come too.
